# Review Interactions

For alleged missing configuration controls, check the supplied evidence for relevant neighbouring editors before accepting absence. Require only the control type and location demanded by the source. Legend-band colour editing does not by itself prove selection of a configured colouring metric. Compare actual saved legend colours with settled map points and disclose any additional refresh needed; a working editor alone cannot pass unrelated preservation checks.

Verify the exact view and data level named by the source. A map's test-selection or cluster-member list is not automatically the selected record's detail/sample table. For sorting/filtering claims, name the panel, its row entities and the matching criterion-owned baseline/action/final captures. If only another table was exercised, mark the required behaviour Unverified until its intended scope is established; do not pass it because the other table sorted correctly or call the required control missing without inspecting that view.

For a disabled-control or prevented-selection claim, `aria-disabled`, a disabled DOM property, grey styling and a tooltip describe state or intent; none alone proves that activation is prevented. Require the criterion-owned target identity and qualifying condition, a settled before capture, recorded normal pointer activation and a settled after capture showing whether details/navigation/selection changed. Require the normal keyboard activation path too when the application exposes one; do not invent a keyboard requirement for a non-focusable row. A tool timeout or refusal to click is not proof that a user attempted activation. Do not use forced clicks, change attributes or bypass the disabled state. If normal activation opens the prohibited details, mark Failed. If the interaction sequence is missing or inconclusive, mark Unverified; use Blocked only for an actual unavailable prerequisite. Name the exact missing proof rather than accepting the tester's disabled label.

Assess the stated scope, not additional imagined coverage. An empty initial list is a valid baseline for unchanged-state and no-new-item assertions unless populated entries are required by the source or inherently by the asserted action. Verify its settled empty baseline and all asserted intermediate/final states; describe "remained empty", without claiming populated ordering coverage. A missing panel alone or failed loading does not prove emptiness; use Unverified for insufficient proof rather than inventing a missing-fixture blocker. Reordering/removal and explicit populated-list assertions still require entries. Visible catalogue restoration needs the initial visible viewport and matching final view, not off-screen entries unless the criterion requires them. Independent review may identify new gaps, but every gap must trace to the same canonical criterion and scope, not a new requirement.

For sample-to-detail navigation, require readable selected-row and opened-detail identity/time at the precision the application actually displays. An application tooltip or expanded title captured in the criterion's images is valid visible proof. Do not demand hidden millisecond precision; use the recorded request only as supporting correlation, not as a substitute for absent visible destination evidence. Distinguish a clipped screenshot from a proven wrong destination. For table sorting, assess each required column separately; one sorted column cannot prove all. Derive the required columns from the source: a criterion asking for one supported column does not require all columns. Review every criterion and its decisive evidence, but do not invent extra test branches or request screenshots of routine setup that proves no source requirement. Missing reachable captures are Unverified, while directly demonstrated missing required controls or incorrect ordering are Failed. Chart series must be shown on the chart, not inferred from separate sample-table values.

Defaults, before/after comparisons, persistence and absence-of-control claims require evidence of each relevant state. Configured-default claims additionally require an independent baseline; a changed value alone is insufficient. Missing baseline/state makes the criterion Unverified, with the gap named.

For sequences such as toggle, restore, close and reopen, require this criterion's own ordered screenshots and steps to show the complete journey. Never assemble a pass from separate criterion runs. Missing transitions are Unverified. Keep reasons concise and describe what evidence shows, not boilerplate about file presence or expected hosts.

For unchanged map/layer claims, inspect the original unobscured baseline and restored final state, including every list-scroll capture needed to see the complete list. Name the decisive baseline/action/final filenames in the criterion reason. For repeated edits, distinguish the specific first/second/final sequence being asserted from earlier setup; describe that decisive sequence rather than reciting all setup. The publisher must retain comparison baselines beneath their criterion; unrelated setup belongs in the compact supporting appendix or full local attempt, not in place of the proof.

Treat “remains the same” and “clearing restores the original” as preservation/restoration comparisons, not just final-state assertions. When preservation applies during searching, inspect the intermediate search state too. For a single unsaved Username cancellation, identify both the entered-value capture and the reopened empty-field capture; the publisher pairs the decisive states beneath the criterion. Do not shorten repeated edits or omit any state required by the assertion.
